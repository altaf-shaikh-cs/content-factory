Four questions to audit yourself against in 2026, from Andrew Ng's read of 10,000 job postings on which software skills actually matter now.

1. Can you build and deploy an AI application, and control it. Normal software is predictable. AI isn't, the same question can get two different answers. The skill is evals and error analysis, not the demo.

2. Do you still know your fundamentals. Every software decision is a tradeoff: fast, secure, cheap. An AI will make that tradeoff for you if you just say "make it good." You won't know what it picked until something breaks.

3. Can you use a coding agent well. Know its limits. Know how much to trust it. One bad instruction can take down a production database. And do it efficiently, time and tokens both count.

4. Are you shaping what gets built, or just taking orders. Deciding which problem is worth solving is now part of the job, not a bonus.

Coding used to be the whole job. Now it's the floor everything else stands on.

You used to be the bricklayer. Now a robot lays bricks faster than you ever could. Your value was never the bricklaying.

Of the four, which one are you actually building, and which one are you just hoping the agent handles for you?
