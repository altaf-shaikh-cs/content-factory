Reading 25 tips about Claude Code will not make you better at Claude Code.

I know this because I've read them. I've saved them. I've bookmarked threads on r/ClaudeAI with hundreds of upvotes from people who've used the tool daily for 11 months. I've nodded along to every one.

And then I've gone back to working the same way I was before.

The thing that actually moved the needle wasn't a tip. It was volume. The more sessions I ran, the more I started noticing where things broke down. Context degrading halfway through a complex refactor. Having to re-explain decisions I'd already made an hour ago. Losing the thread of a problem because I hadn't broken it into sub-tasks that fit the session window.

I didn't read my way to understanding that. I ran into it so many times I had no choice but to fix it.

Someone on Reddit calls this the billion-token rule: the engineers who get the most out of these tools aren't the ones who read the most about them. They're the ones who've done enough reps that the lessons are no longer advice. They're instincts.

A tips list is a shortcut to the vocabulary. It's not a shortcut to the intuition. The intuition comes from the reps.

The tips you'll actually keep are the ones you rediscovered yourself, after you ran into the same wall three times.
