## Core Insight

The reason your AI feels mediocre is not the model, it's that you hired a brilliant new teammate and never onboarded them, and the four features everyone ignores are exactly the onboarding.

## Target Audience

Early-to-mid career engineers and senior ICs in India's tech hubs (Mumbai, Bengaluru, Delhi, Hyderabad) who already pay for an AI coding assistant, use it in default configuration, and quietly suspect they are getting less out of it than the people who post about it.

## Structure

1. The onboarding gap: a capable new hire with zero context is capable and useless. That is a default AI setup, not a model limitation.
2. What onboarding actually consists of: standards that persist (Skills), a pre-kitted workspace (Plugins), walls between bodies of work (Projects), real deliverables instead of chat text (Artifacts). Translated to engineering work, not explained as a feature list.
3. The shift this produces: it stops being a thing you chat with and becomes an environment configured to how you work.
4. The smallest real first step: one workflow that eats time every week, one Skill for it, then the next one.

## CTA

If you have built one custom Skill you actually reuse, tell me what it does. I want to see what other builders automated first.

## Tone Range

Conversational, builder-honest, lightly provocative. No lecture voice. The reader should feel recognized, not scolded.

## Performance Context

- Best performing angle so far: Contrarian 7.43% (n=1), Story-first 6.39% avg (n=2), Bold claim-first 3.86% avg (n=3).
- Hook styles that drove comments: indictments the reader half-recognizes about themselves (ai-drinks-water 7.43%), and specific recognizable developer frustration (gen-ai-roadmap 7.69%).
- Hook styles that drove passive reactions only: scene-setting openers and "I built a system" process framing (routines: 848 impressions, 2.48%). High reach, weak conversion.
- Topics/themes that resonated: practical skill-building, roadmaps, non-obvious dev-tool insight, and A vs B comparisons where the reader has a stake.
- What to avoid repeating: the Contrarian indictment hook already used on this exact idea 2026-07-19; generic hashtag clusters; any external link in the body (links go to first comment).
- Implication for THIS post: keep the uncomfortable recognition that made Contrarian work, but relocate it from "your company is fake-adopting AI" to "you skipped onboarding your own assistant", which is personal, actionable, and structurally a comparison. Open fast, no scene-setting.

## Angle Assignments

- **Angle A: Story-first** — open with the recognizable moment of re-explaining the same context to a fresh chat, let the onboarding insight emerge.
- **Angle B: Bold claim-first** — lead with the claim that the model is not the bottleneck, the configuration is, then back it with the four pieces.
- **Angle C: Comparison/split-screen-first** — bake both sides into the opening line: same model, two setups, wildly different output.

Chose Angle C because decision rule 3 matched: the idea is structurally a comparison, day-one capable-but-useless versus configured, and the source's own summary is "chatbot" versus "configurable work environment". Rule 1 did not match (no reversed-belief moment in the source) and rule 4 did not match (the only real number is "four"). Comparison also aligns with the tracker signal that A vs B framings where the reader has a stake outperform, and it is the furthest structural distance from the Contrarian take already shipped on this idea.
