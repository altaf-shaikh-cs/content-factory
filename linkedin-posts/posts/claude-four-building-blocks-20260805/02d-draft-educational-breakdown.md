Skills, Plugins, Projects, Artifacts.

Four Claude features, used interchangeably in most conversations, doing four completely different jobs. Here is the clean version.

Skills are instructions Claude reads on its own.

You write down how you work in plain text: your format, your conventions, the checks you run before anything ships. Claude loads the relevant one when a task fits it, in every conversation, without being reminded. There are built-in ones and a partner directory with tools like Notion, Figma, and Atlassian. The ones that change your day are the ones you write for your own work.

Plugins are Skills plus everything around them, packaged.

One install carries a bundle: Skills, connectors to your other software, slash commands, sub-agents. Anthropic open-sourced 11 of them and there is a marketplace. This is the unit you use when you want to hand your entire setup to someone else, or pick up someone else's.

Projects are the boundary.

A workspace holding the context, files, and history for one body of work, and not sharing them with the next one. Team and Enterprise plans add two permission tiers, who can use a project and who can edit it.

Artifacts are the output.

Documents, decks, tables, interactive tools, small apps, produced as real files instead of text in a chat. You preview, edit, download, or share by link, and on individual plans the person receiving it does not need an account.

The short version, if you only remember one line each:

Same standard applied every time, that is Skills.
A whole working setup in one install, that is Plugins.
Two bodies of work that never touch, that is Projects.
Something you can actually send to someone, that is Artifacts.

Consistency, capability, separation, output. Four jobs, four tools, and now you know which is which.

If you have built one custom Skill you actually reuse, tell me what it does. I want to see what other builders automated first.
