You are still typing instructions Claude could have remembered permanently.

That is the whole pitch for Skills, and it is the one feature to learn before any of the others.

A Skill is your way of working written down once, in plain text. How you format things. What you never touch. The checklist you run before anything ships. Claude picks the right one up automatically when a task fits it, and it persists across every conversation, so you stop re-explaining yourself to a fresh chat.

There are built-in ones, and a partner directory with tools like Notion, Figma, and Atlassian. The real power is the custom ones. Nobody else can write the Skill for how your team works.

Then the other three, which most people never turn on either.

Plugins: a whole way of working in one install. Skills bundled with connectors, commands, and sub-agents. Anthropic open-sourced 11 of them and there is a marketplace now. Someone joins, installs one, and the AI already knows how you work.

Projects: walls between bodies of work. Context, files, and history stay inside one space, so what you did an hour ago stops bleeding into what you are doing now. If anything you touch is confidential, this is not a nice-to-have.

Artifacts: output that survives the conversation. Documents, decks, tables, interactive tools, small apps. Real files you can edit, download, and share by link, and on individual plans the person you send it to does not need an account. The most useful of the four day to day, and the one nobody mentions.

Skills are consistency. Plugins are capability. Projects are separation. Artifacts are output.

Start with Skills. Pick the workflow that eats your week and write one for it today.

If you have built one custom Skill you actually reuse, tell me what it does. I want to see what other builders automated first.
