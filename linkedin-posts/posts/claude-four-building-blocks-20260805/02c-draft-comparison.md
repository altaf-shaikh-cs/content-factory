Same model, same subscription, two engineers. One of them says AI writes code like their team wrote it. The other says it is fine, but you still have to fix everything.

Nothing separates them except configuration.

It is the difference between a new hire on day one and the same new hire after a week of onboarding. Identical person. They can read, reason, write, ship. On day one they still do not know your conventions, your structure, or what not to touch. Capable and useless.

Four things close that gap, and most people never turn any of them on.

Skills are your conventions written once and picked up automatically whenever a task fits. Not pasted into a fresh chat every morning.

Plugins are the whole setup in one install. Skills, connectors, commands, sub-agents, bundled. Someone joins, installs it, and the AI already knows how you work.

Projects are walls between bodies of work, so one context stops leaking into the next.

Artifacts are output that survives the conversation. Docs, decks, tables, small working tools you can actually hand to someone.

Consistency. Capability. Separation. Output.

The engineer who says AI is overrated and the one who says it changed how they build are usually running the same model. One of them onboarded it.

Pick the workflow that eats your week. Write one Skill for it. Then the next one.
