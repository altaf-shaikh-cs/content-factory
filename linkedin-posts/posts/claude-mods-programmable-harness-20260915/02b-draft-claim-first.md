A Tetris mod is not the story. That it shipped on day one is.

Claude Code opened its mod system this week. Within hours someone had eight arcade games running in the strip above the prompt, plus a virtual pet that gains XP when your tests pass and loses mood when they fail.

508,000 views on the announcement.

Directly below it on the same trending page, a different verdict: "It's been 23 days since I last opened Claude Code." He prefers Codex now, because it works better with the open models he wants to run.

Those two posts look like opposites. They are the same observation.

Neither one is an argument about model quality. One person left because the harness would not bend to the models he wanted. The other stayed and bent the harness until it ran Tetris.

The model is the commodity now. The harness is the product.

Here is the detail that proves it: the arcade costs zero tokens. Not cheap, zero. The plugin answers every keypress itself, because a falling block is deterministic code and always was.

That is the lesson worth carrying into whatever you are building.

The model call is the slowest, most expensive and least reliable thing in your system. Every piece of work you can push out of it and into ordinary code gets faster, free and exact.

Most teams are still deciding which model to use. The sharper question is how much of the product needs one at all.

What in your stack still goes through a model call that ordinary code would do better?
