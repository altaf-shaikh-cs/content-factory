Same trending page. Same 23 hours. Two opposite verdicts on the same tool.

Top post: Claude Code shipped a mod system, and someone had already built Tetris inside it. 508,000 views.

The post directly below it: "It's been 23 days since I last opened Claude Code." He prefers Codex now, because it works better with the open models he wants to run.

I almost scrolled past both. A toy and a churn complaint.

Then I noticed that neither one is arguing about how good the model is.

The leaver left over the harness: which models it will run, how it fits his setup.

The builder stayed because the harness became programmable. He shipped eight games into the strip above the prompt, a pet that gains XP when your tests pass and loses mood when they fail, and a picker that opens the pet after a failed test and a longer game during a long turn.

Model quality decided neither one.

The model is the commodity now. The harness is the product.

And here is the detail that proves it: the arcade costs zero tokens. Not cheap, zero. The plugin answers every keystroke itself, because a falling block is deterministic code and never needed a model.

I made the same call in my own pipeline. The video step is plain code, not a generative model. A model cannot reliably spell a statistic, and the statistic is the entire point of the video. Cheaper was not the reason. Exact was.

Most teams are still deciding which model to use. The sharper question is how much of the product needs one at all.

What in your stack still goes through a model call that ordinary code would do better?
